## Project name

AstroTwin: A Personal Health Digital Twin for Astronauts

## High-level summary

On the way to Mars, help from Earth is up to 20 minutes away, so astronauts must spot health problems themselves. AstroTwin is a health monitoring dashboard that learns each astronaut's personal normal from a 1-minute daily check-in. Machine learning (rolling personal baselines plus an Isolation Forest) flags unusual days, and five gauges track NASA's key risks: bone, cardiovascular, immune, behavioral and radiation. Real solar flares and particle events from NASA's DONKI database trigger shelter alerts. Every alert comes with plain-language actions. On a simulated 180-day Mars mission, AstroTwin caught 8 of 8 hidden health events with a 2% false-alarm rate.

## Project details

**What it does**
AstroTwin turns a 1-minute daily check-in (resting heart rate, blood pressure, sleep, exercise, mood, symptoms) into early warnings. Instead of comparing astronauts to a population average, it compares each person with their own previous 14 days. Heart rate of 66 bpm may be normal for one crew member and a warning sign for another.

**How it works**
1. Personal baselines: rolling z-scores per astronaut and per measurement, using only past days.
2. Anomaly detection: one Isolation Forest per astronaut finds days that are unusual across several measurements at once. A flag is kept only when at least one measurement is clearly outside the personal normal.
3. Risk scores (0–100) for five NASA human-health risk areas: bone loss, cardiovascular change, immune stress, behavioral health and radiation exposure.
4. Space weather: real solar flares, geomagnetic storms and solar energetic particle events from NASA DONKI are placed on the mission timeline and trigger shelter alerts.
5. Guidance: each alert says what to do next, for example rest and re-check, protect the next sleep period, or book a private call home.

**Results**
We hid 8 health events (illness, insomnia, heart-rate spikes) across 13 days in a simulated 180-day mission for a crew of 4. AstroTwin caught all 8 events and 12 of 13 bad days, with 13 false alarms in 664 monitored days (2.0%). We tuned it to miss nothing: on a Mars mission, a missed illness costs more than an extra check-in.

**Mission replay**
A slider moves through all 180 days. Mission day 90 is 10 May 2024, when NASA DONKI recorded X-class solar flares and a burst of energetic particles. The dashboard shows the shelter alert, the radiation gauge jumping, the month-4 behavioral slump and bone loss building over six months.

**Benefits**
Astronauts get early, personal warnings without waiting for Earth. The same approach helps people on Earth who live far from a doctor: polar and submarine crews, remote workers, and older adults living alone.

**Tools**
Python, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, Plotly, Streamlit, requests. Deployed on Streamlit Community Cloud. Code is open source on GitHub.

**Limitations**
Astronaut health data is simulated because real records are private. Risk weights are hand-set. Next steps: test on public wearable datasets and calibrate risk scores with clinical input.

## Use of NASA data and resources

We used NASA's DONKI (Space Weather Database Of Notifications, Knowledge, Information), served by NASA's Community Coordinated Modeling Center (ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/DONKI-API). We downloaded all solar flares (FLR), geomagnetic storms (GST) and solar energetic particle events (SEP) from 11 February to 8 August 2024, our simulated mission window. Each real event is placed on the mission timeline: high-severity events (X-class flares, particle events, Kp 8+ storms) trigger a shelter alert and raise radiation risk. The challenge's description of long-duration health risks (radiation, isolation, altered gravity, closed environment) shaped our five risk areas. Radiation risk is measured against NASA's 600 mSv career limit.

## Space agency partner and other data

Simulated astronaut health data, generated by our open-source script (generate_data.py). Trends are based on published spaceflight findings: about 1–1.5% bone loss per month in microgravity, and about 1.8 mSv per day of deep-space radiation, similar to what the Curiosity rover measured during its cruise to Mars.

## Use of artificial intelligence (if the form asks)

The product uses machine learning (scikit-learn Isolation Forest) for anomaly detection. The team also used an AI assistant (Claude) to help plan the project and write and debug code. All results were run, checked and reviewed by the team.
